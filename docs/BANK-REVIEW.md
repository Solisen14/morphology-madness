## Four decisions for educator review

1. **Language forms:** Are we comfortable including both ordinary English prefixes/suffixes and Greek/Latin combining forms, with explicit labels?
2. **Spelling rules:** The game still joins letters literally. Do we prefer a spelling-change engine later, or keep teacher notes for exceptions?
3. **Difficulty:** Should advanced vocabulary appear only at Master level, or is seeing unfamiliar cards part of the activity?
4. **Semantic grading:** Keep teacher/learner explanations central. Broad meaning tags are not linguistic proof of valid English formation.

## Teaching cautions

- Not every root in the bank is a bound classical root. Many are complete subject words deliberately included to make creative invention possible.
- A Greek/Latin combining form is not always interchangeable with an English prefix/suffix.
- A string formed by joining three cards is not necessarily an established English word.
- Words may change spelling, pronunciation, grammatical category, or meaning when morphemes combine.
- Meaning Madness checks **broad semantic tags**, not linguistic validity. Word Laboratory is **self-reviewed**.
- Newly added inflectional endings (`-ed`, `-s`, `-es`, `-est`) are kept out of the creative card pool for now; they remain available in the bank.
