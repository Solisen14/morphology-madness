The **Grade 9 Science** choice is a new **school subject** available in the existing three games. The general **Science** subject remains unchanged. This is not a separate game or a replacement for science instruction.

## Editorial principles

1. Each real-word answer must be reconstructible from at least **two distinct, meaning-bearing parts**. It can use prefix + base, base + suffix, or all three.
2. Exact spelling is automatically checked. Spelling-based shortcuts and arbitrary syllable divisions do not qualify.
3. Greek/Latin **bound roots** and combining forms are explicitly labelled. They are meaningful, but are not necessarily standalone English words.
4. Vocabulary includes some elementary prerequisite terms and a handful of scientifically related extension words. This does not imply every term is a mandatory SNC1W expectation.
5. Important science words that do not divide naturally into two parts, such as *atom*, *mass*, or *orbit*, may be included as a **root** for constructing longer words, but are not automatically standalone Word Wizard questions.
6. The three existing modes, difficulty options, student privacy characteristics, and overall site layout remain unchanged.
7. Meaning Madness accepts creative semantic interpretations and student self-reflections, not teacher-graded or AI-graded responses.

## Teaching consideration

The strongest learning use is to have students **explain how each morpheme contributes to the meaning**, then connect the constructed word to a scientific model, observation, or phenomenon. Being able to assemble the correct letters alone does not demonstrate full conceptual understanding.

All entries and explanations are newly written for this activity. They have not been approved by the Ministry of Education.

### Word-part quality review (v1.8)

Six Science challenges now use three informative parts: `observation`, `decomposer`, `producer`, `adaptation`, `conductor`, and `conductivity`. For example, the Latin historical structure behind *conductor* can be taught as **con- + duct + -or**, while the modern scientific meaning must still be explained explicitly. Etymology informs vocabulary learning but is not itself a definition of a physics concept.
