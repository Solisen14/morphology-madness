# Grade 9 English vocabulary (Ontario ENL1W, 2023)

**Grade 9 English** is a separate School Subject in the three existing Morphology Madness modes. The general **English** selection stays available. This is not a fourth game or a prescribed course vocabulary list.

## Authoritative curriculum reference

[Ontario Ministry of Education: English, Grade 9, De-streamed, ENL1W (2023)](https://www.dcp.edu.gov.on.ca/en/curriculum/secondary-english/courses/enl1w/course-intro).

ENL1W organizes learning into four integrated strands. Strand **A** is overarching and is taught and assessed through the learning in strands **B–D**. Our A–D labels are **editorial categories for teachers**, not official Ministry word lists or claims that every entry is mandated.

Word Laboratory draws from **110** eligible roots or bases: **54 new Grade 9 roots**, **50 shared with general English**, and **6 reused from other subjects**. It uses the existing shared prefix and suffix reels plus six new prefixes/combining forms and four suffixes.

## Word selection and assessment

- All 99 Word Wizard answers are real, relevant terms that students can reconstruct from **at least two meaning-bearing components**; exact spelling and availability are verified in automated tests.
- A meaningful base can be a free word or a bound classical root; it is not necessarily a standalone English word. The new **ant-** and **hom-** initial forms in *antonym* and *homonym* account for differences from the forms *anti-* and *homo-* in other words.
- A few advanced literary terms (*introspection*, *retrospective*, *intertextual*) are useful extensions, not required vocabulary for all Grade 9 students.
- Word Wizard checks the target construction. Meaning Madness invites creative meanings and explanation but does not reliably assess original inventions. Word Laboratory requires students to make meaning from the component parts.
- A word part's etymology helps with vocabulary, but teachers should still explicitly teach contemporary meanings and spelling conventions. The game alone does not measure reading comprehension or writing proficiency.
- The existing English subject, other subjects, game mechanics, colour scheme, and student data practices are unchanged.
- Following our existing design decision, these notes live in GitHub documentation rather than adding extra text to the student-facing website.

**Teaching tip:** Invite students to explain *why* a word's pieces suggest its meaning, then provide a text-based example in context. Students may need direct teaching of classical roots and of spelling conventions that do not always match transparent modern-English combinations.
